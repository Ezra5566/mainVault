**Definition**  
Autonomous systems are AI-driven entities that can perceive their environment, make decisions, and execute actions without human intervention. They operate based on pre‑programmed objectives, learned policies, or real‑time adaptive algorithms.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Perception** – Sensor fusion (camera, lidar, GPS, etc.) to understand surroundings.  
- **Decision‑Making** – Planning, strategy generation, or policy selection (often via reinforcement learning).  
- **Action** – Interaction with the world (e.g., controlling a robot, navigating a vehicle).  
- **Adaptivity** – Ability to adjust behavior in response to changes or new data.

**Representative Examples**
- **Self‑Driving Cars** – Perceive traffic, plan trajectories, control steering/braking.  
- **Industrial Robots** – Perform assembly tasks, inspect products, adapt to line changes.  
- **Drones & UAVs** – Navigate autonomously, avoid obstacles, complete missions.  
- **Autonomous Warehouse Agents** – Pick, pack, and manage inventory without human operators.  
- **Chatbots & Virtual Assistants** – Process user input, generate responses, take actions (e.g., bookings).

**Relevance to AI Foundations**
- Demonstrates **real‑world application** of narrow AI.  
- Highlights importance of **generalization** across unpredictable environments.  
- Brings up **safety**, **reliability**, and **ethical** considerations (e.g., decision‑making in critical scenarios).  
- Connects to **reinforcement learning**, **computer vision**, **natural language processing**, and **multi‑modal perception**.

**Discussion Prompt for Students**  
- “What are the societal implications of widespread autonomous systems?”  
- “How should accountability be assigned when an autonomous system causes harm?”
