title: What AI Is vs What It Is Not
tags: [ai-foundations, week1, intro]
week: 1
---

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to systems that can perform tasks that typically require human intelligence—such as reasoning, learning, perception, and decision‑making. However, AI is **not**:

- General human-like consciousness or sentience.  
- A guarantee of correctness; AI can produce biased or erroneous outputs.  
- Always autonomous; many AI systems assist rather than replace humans.  

**Key distinctions**  
- **AI vs. Traditional Software:** AI often learns from data rather than following static rules.  
- **AI vs. Human Expertise:** AI augments human capability but lacks understanding, intent, and general reasoning.  

**Everyday examples students already encounter**  
- Recommendation engines (streaming services, search engines)  
- Virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa, ChatGPT)  
- Spam filters, traffic‑pattern predictions  

This foundational view sets the stage for differentiating narrow AI, general AI, and generative AI in later sections.
