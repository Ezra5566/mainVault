**Definition**  
Generative AI refers to models that can create new content—such as text, images, audio, or video—based on patterns learned from training data. Unlike discriminative models that classify or predict, generative models *produce* original outputs.

**Key Characteristics**  
- **Creativity**: Synthesizes never‑seen‑before examples.  
- **Prompt‑driven**: Output controlled by user‑provided prompts.  
- **Probabilistic**: Samples from learned distributions, leading to variability.

**Typical Models (2026)**  
- Large Language Models (LLMs) – e.g., GPT‑4‑Turbo, Mistral‑7B‑Instruct.  
- Diffusion Models – image, audio, and video synthesis.  
- Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) – image generation and style transfer.

**Major Application Areas**  
- **Chatbots & Virtual Assistants** – natural‑language dialogue.  
- **Content Creation** – article drafts, code generation, design mockups.  
- **Data Augmentation** – synthetic data for training other ML models.  
- **Scientific Simulations** – generating plausible molecular structures or lab results.

**Ethical Considerations**  
- Potential for misinformation and deepfakes.  
- Copyright and attribution of AI‑generated works.  
- Bias propagation in generated content.

**Takeaway**  
Generative AI is reshaping how we interact with technology, turning tools into collaborators that can ideate, design, and produce content autonomously.
