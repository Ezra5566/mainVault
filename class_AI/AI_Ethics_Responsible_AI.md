# AI Ethics & Responsible AI

**Why this module is essential:** Universities increasingly require graduates to understand the societal impact of AI systems. This module equips students with the critical lenses needed to design, evaluate, and deploy AI responsibly.

## Core Topics

| Area | Key Concepts | Practical Implications |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| **Bias** | - Sources of bias (data, measurement, algorithmic) <br> - Fairness metrics (demographic parity, equal opportunity) <br> - Mitigation strategies (pre‑processing, in‑process, post‑processing) | Detecting skewed predictions in hiring or credit‑scoring models; designing debiasing pipelines |
| **Privacy** | - Differential privacy fundamentals <br> - Data anonymization vs. pseudonymization <br> - Legal frameworks (GDPR, CCPA) | Building privacy‑preserving recommendation systems; handling sensitive health data |
| **Hallucinations** | - Definition and causes (over‑confidence, training gaps) <br> - Detection techniques (confidence calibration, entailment checking) <br> - Mitigation (retrieval‑augmented generation, verification layers) | Preventing fabricated medical diagnoses; ensuring factual consistency in chatbot answers |
| **Explainability** | - Model‑agnostic vs. model‑specific methods (SHAP, LIME, counterfactuals) <br> - Importance of interpretability for trust and regulation <br> - Communicating explanations to non‑technical stakeholders | Providing clinicians with feature importance scores; building audit trails for credit decisions |
| **AI Governance** | - Ethical AI principles (human oversight, accountability, transparency) <br> - Institutional AI policies and review boards <br> - Standards and certifications (ISO/IEC 42001) | Setting up an AI ethics committee; implementing policy‑driven model lifecycle management |
| **Academic Integrity** | - Proper citation of AI‑generated content <br> - Detecting plagiarism with AI‑aware tools <br> - Designing assignments resilient to LLM assistance | Crafting project briefs that require original analysis; teaching students to attribute AI output |
| **Discussion Prompt** | *“Should AI replace exams?”* – debate format, evidence‑based argumentation, policy recommendation writing | Encourages critical thinking, policy synthesis, and communication skills |

## Suggested Activities

- **Case‑Study Analysis** – Small groups dissect a recent AI‑related news story (e.g., biased facial‑recognition deployment). Prepare a briefing that outlines the ethical failure, root causes, and remediation steps.
- **Ethics Debate** – Formal debate on whether AI should be granted legal personhood. Teams must cite legal precedents, ethical theories, and technical limitations.
- **Reflective Essay** – 500‑word position paper on “The role of explainability in high‑stakes AI domains.” Include at least one concrete example (e.g., medical diagnosis).
- **Policy‑Mockup** – Draft a one‑page AI governance charter for a fictional startup. Include sections on data stewardship, bias monitoring, and incident reporting.
- **Hands‑On Lab** – Use an open‑source bias‑detection toolkit (e.g., IBM AI Fairness 360) to audit a pre‑trained classifier on a public dataset.

## Assessment Ideas

1. **Written Assignment** – 10 % of final grade; students critique an AI system’s ethical risks and propose a mitigation plan (max 2 pages).
2. **Group Presentation** – 15 % of final grade; 10‑minute presentation of an AI ethics case study with Q&A.
3. **Peer Review** – Participants review a classmate’s policy mockup, providing constructive feedback (graded on thoroughness and insight).

## Recommended Readings & Resources

- **Books**: *“Weapons of Math Destruction”* – O’Neil; *“Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans”* – Melanie Mitchell.
- **Papers**:  
  - “Fairness through Awareness” (Dwork et al., 2012)  
  - “Explainable AI: Exploring Methods and Applications” (LIME, SHAP overview)  
  - “Differential Privacy for Machine Learning” (Abadi et al., 2016)
- **Online Courses**:  
  - Coursera *“AI Ethics”* (University of Helsinki)  
  - edX *“Ethics of AI and Big Data”* (Columbia University)
- **Tools**:  
  - IBM AI Fairness 360 (open‑source bias mitigation)  
  - Google’s What‑If Tool (model inspection)  
  - Hugging Face’s `evaluate` library (metrics for hallucination detection)

> **Tip for Instructors:** Integrate ethical checkpoints into each technical project (e.g., require a bias analysis section in the final report). This reinforces responsible practice without treating ethics as an afterthought.
